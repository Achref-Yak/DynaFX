The trial court erred in excluding the expert testimony under Daubert.
The expert holds a PhD in biomechanics and has published 12 peer-reviewed
studies on whiplash kinematics. Peer review establishes reliability.
However, the expert's methodology deviated from the standard protocol
in this specific case — she used a 30-degree seatback angle instead of
the standard 25 degrees. The opposing expert, who followed the standard
protocol, reached a different conclusion. This contradicts the notion
that the testimony was unreliable. Since the case involves a federal
question, the Federal Rules of Evidence apply. Under Rule 702, expert
testimony is admissible if it is "based on sufficient facts or data."
The deviation may affect weight, not admissibility. FRE 702 further
requires that the testimony be "the product of reliable principles and
methods." This was satisfied. The judgment should be reversed.
