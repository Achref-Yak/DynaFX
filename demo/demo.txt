The AI feature will increase user retention by 15% within six months. The beta test with 500 users showed a 12% lift in weekly active users, supporting this projection. However, the beta only included power users who are more likely to adopt new features, which may skew results. The engineering team argues that the feature introduces unacceptable latency — our competitor analysis shows users abandon tools that exceed 200ms response time. This is a valid constraint, not just an opinion. The architecture review confirmed the AI model adds 300ms overhead, which directly contradicts the latency requirement. One reviewer claimed "users will tolerate slower responses for better AI," but this confuses a wish with an empirical fact. In fact, the support ticket analysis shows a 40% increase in complaints when response time exceeds 200ms. The product manager justified the feature by arguing that the retention gain outweighs the latency cost, framing it as a trade-off between engagement and performance. The real issue is a category mistake: treating a hard performance constraint as negotiable. A fallback to a cached model reduces overhead to 50ms, which satisfies both requirements.
